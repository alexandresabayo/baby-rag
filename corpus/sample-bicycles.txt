A Short History of the Bicycle

The first verified practical bicycle, the Laufmaschine or draisine, was invented by Baron
Karl von Drais in 1817 in Mannheim, Germany. It had no pedals; the rider pushed the ground
with their feet.

Pedals attached directly to the front wheel appeared with the velocipede in the 1860s in
Paris. The high-wheeler penny-farthing followed in the 1870s; its enormous front wheel
made it fast but dangerous.

The safety bicycle of 1885, with equal-sized wheels and a chain drive, and the pneumatic
tyre invented by John Boyd Dunlop in 1888, turned the bicycle into transport for everyone.
