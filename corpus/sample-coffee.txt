How Coffee Changed the World

Coffee is believed to have been discovered in Ethiopia, where legend tells of the goat
herder Kaldi noticing his goats' excitement after eating red berries from a certain shrub.

By the fifteenth century, coffee was cultivated in Yemen and drunk in Sufi monasteries.
From Mocha it spread to Cairo, Damascus and Istanbul, and then to Europe through Venetian
merchants in the seventeenth century.

Coffee houses became centres of conversation, business and politics; London's coffee
houses famously gave rise to Lloyd's of London, the insurance market. Today coffee is one
of the most traded agricultural commodities on Earth.
